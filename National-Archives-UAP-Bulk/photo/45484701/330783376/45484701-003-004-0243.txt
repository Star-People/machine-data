AT NEN YORK, NEW YORK
Mr. J. I. SACKETT, telephonically contacted this office on 4 February 1953
to report that he had observed a "flying saucer" in Guatemala, Central America,
and desired to turn over his notes concerning the incident.
On 5 February 1953, the writer interviewed Mr. JOHN TOWNSEND SACKET in
Room 2618 of the Savoy Plaza Hotel, Fifth aveme and 58th Street. Mr. SACKETT
identified himself by U.S. passport #452 issued at the U.S. Embassy in
Guatemala, and by numerous cards. SACKETT further advised that he was a zoolo-
gist interested in Natural History, who had been in Guatemala, on and off, since
known in Guatemala as Dr. J. TOWNSEND SACKETT, both by the natives and members
of the U.S. Embassy and military attaches. SACKETT had returned to the United
States to aid his mother who was ill. A letter addressed to Mrs. LEEONARD M.
THOMAS was observed on the writing table of Room 2618.
There also was a ward-
robe trunk in that room with the initials MOT. SACKETT informed the writer that
this room 2618 was the one occupied by his mother.
SACKETT then furnished the writer with a sketch of the incident, which
he had drawn approximately 30 minutes after observing the flying object and with
his notes concerning this episode likewise recorded at that time.
Photostatic
copies of the sketch are attached hereto as Inclosure #l. SACKETT's original
notes and sketch are retained in the files of this office. SACKET was shown
a road map of Guatemala on which he identified his position at the time he ob-
served the incident as being on Route 3, approximately 8-10 kilometers southwest
ward from Guatemala City.
Approximately 90° 38' west longitude and 140 30' north
latitude. SACKETT at that time 1555 hours Central Standard Time or 21552 Satur-
day, 17 January 1953 was on board the 330 bus from Guatemala City to Amatitlan,
and had just entered the rapidly descending portion of Route 3 which was be-
ginning to be very winding. SACKETT observed the flying object through an open
window of the bus, silhouetted against the side of Volcano Fuego, approximately
90° 50' west longitude and 14° 25' north latitude.
SACKETT advised that he, in
navy parlance, was at that time just coming "abeam" of Volcano Fuego. The object

altitude of 6,500 feet - about 2,000 feet above his altitude. Its apparent direc-
tion of flight was north-westerly. A photostatic copy of this map with the two
