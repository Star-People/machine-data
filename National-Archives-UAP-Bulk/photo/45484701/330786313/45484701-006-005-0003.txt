AT YUMA AIR FORCE BASE, ARIZONA
On 28 August 1952, S/Sgt JOSEPH S.
VLIN, AF19362727, senior
control tower operator and assistant control tower chief, 1903-4 AACS
Detachment, 4750th Air Base Group, Yuma County Municipal Airport, Yuma,
Arizona, was interviewed by reporting Agent.
Upon interview MAYLIN stated that he was on duty alone in the
control tower at Yuma Air Force Base on 13 May 1952, and at 0400 hours the
El Centro, tower reported seeing six (6) bright objects moving to the
southeast and asked MAYLIN to try to spot them. MAYLIN advised that he
scanned the sky with 7 x 50 binoculars and at 0450 hours sighted two (2)
bright orange objects at 300 degrees true. MATLIN advised further that
these objects could not be seen with the naked eye and that they appeared
to be about one (1) foot in diameter and appeared to be round. MAYLIN
further advised that the objects appeared to be at an altitude of from
2500 to 5000 feet. MAILIN also stated that he watched the objects for
about thirty (30) minutes and they were moving back and forth across the
sky from 290 degrees to about 305 degrees, as indicated on the crash map,
and appeared to be getting further away all the time until finally they
disappeared. MAYLIN advised that a beam of light similar to that of a
search light came from one of the objects and that the beam of light kept
going on and off in different directions from the object. MAYLIN also
advised that very short but continuous trails of what appeared to be flame
came out of both objects. MAYLIN further advised that it was dark when he
first sighted the objects and the weather was very clear.
When asked concerning airline flights, MAYLIN stated that at that
date, Western Air Lines had two flights scheduled daily, one at about 1000
hours and one about 2100 hours.
MAYLIN further advised that when these objects were sighted by him,
no military airoraft from Yuma County Airport or El Centro, California,
were in the air at the time.
MAYLIN stated that the objects were not conventional type aircraft
and that he had never sighted strange aerial objects before or since the
above incident.
MAYIIN advised that to his knowledge no one else at the
base sighted the above objects.
MAYLIN further advised that he had been a flight engineer with
the U.S. Coast Guard from 1943 to 1946 and had acquired approximately
3000 hours flying time during that period. MAYLIN also advised that he had
been a tower operator since ll June 1951.
