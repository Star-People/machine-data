ANGLADO said that he had been on duty between the hours of
2230, 23 May 1951, and 0700 hours, 24 May 1951. ANGLADO explained
that at approximately 2310 hours on 23 May 1951, one (1) of the patrol-
men coming off duty pointed out to him a luminous object extremely
high in the air; the observation was made from the front of the police
station, and there were approximately seven (7) policemen present
noting the object.
ANGLADO said that the object was very high, although
he could not estimate the height in feet, and that the object appeared
to be of the same relative size as the stars. ANGLADO recalled that
there was a full moon, and the sky was not clouded. According to
ANGLADO, the object moved slowly; at times seemed round, at other times
egg-shaped; that it seemed to be rotating; that the object appeared to
be moving in one (1) direction, from the Honolulu Airport toward Hickam
AF Base, and then reversed its path; and that the object disappeared
from sight within approximately fifteen (15) to twenty (20) minutes.
The object was described by ANGLADO as being of a red color, changing
to an orange flame color, and fading to a blue hue. ANGLADO noted no
noise coming from the object, nor could he recall having noted any
particular, separate lights or smoke trails. ANGLADO said that he had
sighted the same or a similar airborne object again at approximately
0515 hours on 24 May 1951; and added that it was just breaking daylight
at that time, and that the object had appeared round and moving in the
one (1) direction from the Honolulu Airport toward Hickam AF Base.
ANGIADO acknowledged that he is twenty-six (26) years of age; that he
has normal (20/20) vision in both eyes; and is not color-blind, that
he served with the U. S. Navy during World War II, with approximately
three (3) years sea duty including aircraft watch; that he has been
employed with the Naval Base Police since November of 1946; and added
that he has previously observed weather balloons aloft, but that the
sightings described did not resemble a weather balloon or any other
object ever sighted before. ANGLADO remarked that one (1) of the
patrolmen who had observed the object made a joke about the object
being a flying saucer.
