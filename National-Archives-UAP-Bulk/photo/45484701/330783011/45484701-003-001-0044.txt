1. During the afternoon of 22 July 1952 Miss Elvira VARGAS, a columnist for the
Mexico City daily newspaper Novedades, telephoned Mr. Barry BISHOP, Press Attaché of
the U.S. Embassy in Mexico City, and told him that she wanted to talk to someone with
regard to some unidentified flying objects that she had seen. Mr. Bishop notified the

2. Miss Vargas related the following experience to Lt. Colonel Hovde and the un-
dersigned. On the night of 19 July 1952, she, accompanied by Antonio PRIETO, a report-
er for the Mexico City daily newspaper El Popular, was walking along Calete Beach at
state of Guerrero, Mexico. Suddenly she observed overhead a very bright
Abject with the size and general eppearance of a bright star traveling at high velocity
from north to south. The light from this object blinked on and off at intervals of
approximately one second. Miss Vargas stated that she had the impression that it was
rolling over and over and that one side of the object was strongly illuminated, the
other side dark. After approximately 30 seconds the object disappeared over the
southern horizon.
3.
About one minute later a second object appeared on the northeast horizon.
This object, also traveling at a high speed, moved across the sky, disappearing over
the southwestern horizon. A third object appeared several minutes later, traveling
northwest to southeast, and was followed by a fourth object about a minute later,
traveling west to east. All of the objects were similar in appearance and behavior
and were visible for about 30 seconds during their journeys from horizon to horizon.
Miss Vargas stated that it appeared that the paths of all of the objects had
crossed at a common point several miles south of the location where she was standing.
A chart of the trajectories of the objects in relation to prominent landmarks is for-
warded as Inclosure No. 1. Miss Vargas remained up most of the night with no success
to see if additional objects could be sighted.
5. The sightings were made between 2215 and 2230 local time, 19 July 1952
(0415% 04302, 20 July 1952) at a location of 16° 511 N; 99° 54' W.
