THE CALDWELL ROTOR PLANE
(Designed to demonstrate the principle of bird flight)
The wings of this plane are pivoted at the center
of pressure; and through means of a cam, attack the air
at the most efficient angle of lift, at both top and
bottom of the path of rotation.
The axles of the rotors are inclined at a negative
or gliding angle in horizontal flight, and are adjusted
to neutral in vertical ascent, and in landing.
In horizontal flight, just enough power will be ex-
pended to maintain altitude. When the lifting impulse
of the rotation compensates for the loss in altitude due
to the coasting tendency of the weight, flight is sus-
tained.
It is by this method that birds have always flown.
By this formula birds are enabled to travel enormous
distances with very little energy.
By adopting this method of flying we expect to
build small passenger planes that will fly fifty and
perhaps one hundred miles on a gallon of gas.
These machines we believe can be handled and oper-
