MEMORANDUM FOR MAJOR GENERAL CARROLL
15 May 1952
SUBJECT :
(Restricted
- Security Information) Unidentified Aerial Object
Sighted General Area Ritchie Highway, South Baltimore, Maryland,
10:45 PM 29 March 1952 by Donald Stewart, 2241 Warren Avenue,
Baltimore, Maryland
1. I thought you might be interested in the results of an investi-
gation we have just completed in regard to the above subject. At approxi-
mately 1600 hours 9 May 1952 we received a telephone call from the
Directorate of Intelligence to the effect that one Donald Stewart of
Baltimore, Maryland, had sighted an unidentified aerial object at 2245
hours 29 March 1952.
According to the information received by AFOIN the
object, when observed by Stewart, was headed in the general direction of
Annapolis and hovered for approximately two (2) minutes 200 feet above
Mr. Stewart's automobile causing the ignition of his automobile to be
magnetized and stopping the engine. Stewart claimed a 17 year old high
school student was present in his automobile at the time of observation
and would corroborate his statement. Stewart had relayed this information
to a news commentator in Baltimore who later told the story to a retired
Army Colonel, who in turn advised AFOIN. Intelligence requested that OSI
afford the matter priority attention since Secretary Finletter had expressed
a personal interest in the report. Accordingly, District Office 4 was in-
structed to conduct a personal interview of Stewart and others who may have
witnessed the alleged object, and to obtain sufficient background information
concerning Stewart to enable an evaluation of his credibility. Upon in-
terview by OSI, Stewart reiterated his previous report. However, our agents
located and interviewed the 17 year old schoolboy who, although at first
confirming the statements as given by Stewart, later admitted that he had
not been with Stewart at the time of the alleged sighting, had not seen any
such aircraft, and had related his previous story at the request of Stewart.
The schoolboy, George Tyler, had hitched a ride with Stewart during the
evening of the alleged incident.
2. Background information developed by DO 4 disclosed that Stewart
had been at one time under investigation for illegally wearing a Navy
uniform, and had also been fired from his employment in Baltimore as a
result of placing a decal of the hammer and sickle and red star on the
door of a Baltimore truck, Stewart was de
eribed by associates as a
braggart and a "screwball".
