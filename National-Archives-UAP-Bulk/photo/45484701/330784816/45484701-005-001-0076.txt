Subject: Sighting of Unconventional Aircraft Over the Carolinas
on 28 December 1949
21 February 1950
Observer Newspaper carried an article on the appearance of the Object and
attributed it to vapor trails from B-36's. During the interview, Dr. JAMES'
figures on the Object's length and degree of height from the horizon agreed
with those given by other witnesses.
Dr. JAMES said though that he believed
the Object was at least 40,000 feet in the sky, andwhile the Object looked
about 20 or 30 feet long, it probably was miles long.
It appeared to this
Agent that Dr. JAMES was far above average in intelligence and ability to
determine. Dr. JAMES also had some knowledge of aeronautics. Reliability
of Dr. JAMS' above statements were unquestioned by this Agent.
7. Upon being interviewed, Mr. BEVERLY BALL, Solicitor, Hamlet Dis-
trict Recorders Court, Hamlet, N. C., stated he observed the Object over
Hamlet, N. C., at about 1630 hours, 28 December 1949, while standing on
Main St. Mr. BALL said that when he first observed the Object it was in
the west, silver in color, looked to be smoke trailing from an airplane,
and was about 20 to 30 degrees above the horizon. After watching it for a
few minutes, the Object seemed to change its shape from a long, thin Object
to that of a cigar or a dirigible. The Object seemed to be about 20 or 30
feet in length and seemed to waver or had a turning motion. Believing the
Object was over the Airport about eight (8) miles west of town, Mr. BALL
said he drove in his car in that direction. When he arrived at the Airport,
he then realized that the Object was a great distance from Hamlet and moving
in the direction of the west. The Object then appeared to Mr. BALL to be
round like a ball. Mr. BALL said the Object was in sight about 40 minutes
and seemed to just be floating away. Mr. BALL said that he observed the
high flying B-36 on 30 December 1949, but the vapor trails left by the B-36
did not appear to be like the Object he saw on 28 December 1949. In regard
to reliability of Mr. BALL, this agent noted Mr. BALL's vision was impaired
by a stilted eye, and he wore glasses.
Mr. BALL holds a responsible position
in Hamlet, N. C., and it is believed that his description of the Object was
a traeful account as to what he saw, however, since no other witness saw the
Object as round, it is believed that Mr. BALL's poor eyesight may have
accounted for this. Mr. BALL had no knowledge of aeronautics, but is a man
of above average intelligence. Mr. BALL drew a sketch of how the Object
appeared to him, and this sketch is inclésed as Inclosure #4. Mr. BALL in-
cluded in this one (1) sketch the three (3) shapes the Object took on during
his observation. The small dotted lines running from one object to another
were Mr. BALL's method of indicating the turning of the Object from one shape
to another.
3. Upon being interviewed, Mr. J. C. WORLE!, Hire Chier, Hamlet, N.C.,
stated the story as did Dr. JAMES except that Chief WORLEY did not offer the
explanations that the Object was the vapor trail of a B-36. Chief WORLEY
was unable to add to the description of the Object as given by Mr. WABBERSON,
HOWARD JAMES, Mr. MARBY, or Dr. JAMES. Chief WORLEY appeared to this agent
to be a man of integrity and of average intelligence.
Chief WORLEY had no
knowledge of aeronautics.
It is believed that Chief WORLEY described the
Object exactly as he saw it.
