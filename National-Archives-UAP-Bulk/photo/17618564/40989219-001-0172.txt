15 CMR, page 664~665, United States v. M/Sgt Howard N. Jones, ACM 7792

Sodomy § 9 —- specification alleging course of conduct rather than Single
acts--affirmance by board of review for violation of Art 134,

1. The accused was convicted of sodomy under a specification which included
allegations that the acts occurred "at divers places at or near Wheelus
Field, Tripoli, and Sabratha, Libya, on divers times during the months

of June and July 1952." HELD; A specification alleging a series of

acts which, considered together constitute a course of conduct of a
particular type is not subject to an objection on the ground of duplicity
irrespective of whether each individual act might constitute a separate
offense. Where such a course of conduct is alleged as a violation of a
specific article, the error may be cured by an amendment of the charge

from the specific article to the general article. Accordingly in the
instant case, although the law officer did not direct amendment of the
charge, there is no reason why the board of review cannot now make such

a Change without prejudice to the substantial rights of the accused thereby
curing the error. (Citing MCM, 1951, par 28b; ACM 6822, Francis, 12 CMR
695 and cases cited therein.)

Sodomy § 20 —~ instructions as to corroboration of victim.

4. Each of the victims testified in detail concerning the offenses and

in spite of vigorous cross-examination persisted in their original testimony.
However, the defense introduced statements made by these victims to the OSI
and the investigating officer prior to trial which contained inconsistencies
as to the dates, places and circumstances pertaining to the offenses charged.
The defense requested the following instruction: "A conviction cannot be
based upon the uncorroborated testimony of an alleged victim in a trial

for a sexual offense, if such testimony is self-contradictory, uncertain or
improbable." This instruction was refused. HELD: ‘The law officer properly
refused to give the above instruction because it was inapplicable inasmuch

as the testimony of the alleged victims was not inconsistent. The nature

of a witness! testimony at the trial determines whether it is to be
characterized as self-contradictory, uncertain or improbable. In other
words, consistent, certain and probable testimony elicited at trial is

not capable of being changed in character by the showing that the witness
made inconsistent statements prior to trial. Such inconsistent statements
are to be considered only in assessing the over-all credibility of the
witness.
