In our opinion, it is the clear expression of the Manual (par 28c), in
harmony with prevailing legal principles, that specifications sufficiently
allege all the elements of the offenses sought to be charged in violation of
Articles 133 or 134, if the specifications comply with the following:

"If, in the light of the general situation existing at the time and
Place alleged, the act described can reasonably be considered as being
unbecoming an officer and a gentleman, as prejudicial to good order and
discipline or as bringing discredit upon the armed forces, then the addi-
tion of an appropriate word importing criminality or wrongfulness is suffi-
cient to apprise the accused that the act charged is alleged to have been
committed under unbecoming circumstances . .." (See Legal and Legislative
Basis, Manual for Courts-Martial, United States, 1951, pp 42-43; CM 307097,
Mellinger, supra; ACM 5819, Hanna, supra; U.S. V. Snyder, supra; U. S.

v. Herndon (No. 570), 1 USCMA 461, 4 CMR 53; OM 348951, Lee, 4 CMR 185;
ACM 8289, Peterson, 16 CMR 565, pet den U.S. v. Peterson, 16, (No. 4231)
16 CMR 292).

Headnote 33

Testing Specification 1 of Additional Charge I and Specification 1 of
Additional Charge II by the rule of the preceding paragraph, we conclude that
an allegation that a married officer occupied a bed with a woman not his wife
on an air base, can reasonably be considered as conduct unbecoming an officer
and a gentleman, discrediting to the service, or prejudicial to good order and
discipline, and that, when accompanied by the additional allegation of the
word "wrongfully," importing criminality, the specification alleges the acts
to have been unbecoming, disorderly, or prejudicial circumstances, depending
upon whether it is charged under Article 133 or Article 134, For the foregoing
reasons, we conclude that Specification 1 of Additional Charge I and Specifica-
tion II contain allegations of the essential elements of the offense sought to
be charged under Articles 134 and 133 respectively.

Headnote 43

Specification 2 of Additional Charge I, alleging an offense of drunk and
disorderly in station, in violation of Article 134, is drawn in model from (see
Specification Form No. 132, App 6c, p 490, MCM, 1951), and it is well settled
that such form of pleading adequately alleges the offense sought to be charged
(see MCM, 1951, pag 28d; ACM 5167, Wheat, 5 CMR 494; U. S. v. Lowe (No. 4620),
4 USCMA 654, 16 CMR 228; ACM 2840, Gunning, 3 CMR (AF) 756; CM 351492, Powers,
5 CMR 206; ACM S-6102, Wallace, 10 CMR 937). As to Specification 2 of Additional
Charge II, alleging in identical language a violation of Article 133, such
allegations, or substantially similar ones, have heretofore been held to be or
treated as sufficient to allege a violation of Article 13% or its antecedent
Articles of War 95 (see CM 232968, McCormick, 19 BR 263; CM 312773, Johnston,
62 BR 289; CM 350729, Akins, 4 CMR 364; CM 355119, Anderson, 8 CMR 212; CM
355287, Loney, 8 CMR 533).

Headnote 5:

The gravamen of an offense in violation of Article 133 (and its antecedent
Articles of War 95) is conduct unbecoming an officer and a gentleman and such
conduct may occur on as well as off a military installation (see Winthrop's
Military Law and Precedents, 2d ed,p 717; MCM, 1951, var 212; CM 347810, Jewson,
7 OMR 213, affd U. S. v Jewson (no. 532), 1 USCMA 652, 5 CMR 80; CM 312773,
Johnston, supra).
