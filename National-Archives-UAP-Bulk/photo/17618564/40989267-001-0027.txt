2. DISCUSSION
2. 1 Heavy-Load Balloons
Under Contract with ONR, a study was established to develop a heavy load
material with the following requirements:
1. High tensile and impact strength.
2. Low service temperature - 100 C.
3. Absolute tear stopping properties at low temperature and room temperature.
4. Weight equivalent to 2-mil poly or less.
5. Forgiveness, a high degree of latitude in handling and fabrication to reduce
the probability of fabrication defects and flight damage. It is impractical to con-
sider that a large plastic balloon can be given 100 percent inspection and, of
course, it is impossible to pretest the balloon prior to flight. Therefore, it is
important to design the reliability into the basic material and balloon design.
This study resulted in what we call the GT-10, GT-11, and GT-12 laminates
consisting of 1/4-mil, 1/3-mil, and 1/2-mil Mylar respectively, laminated to a 12
by 4, two ply fill, 220 denier Dacron leno with GT-301 adhesive system (Figure 1).
This all-polyester film has thermal properties similar to Mylar with added tensile
and tear strength (Figure 2). The GT-10 weighs the same per unit area as 1.8-mil
