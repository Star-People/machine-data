Afterword
An important New Development

On March 30, 1994, I telephoned Glenn Dennis to confirm some
information for the end notes in this report. Dennis did so and then added
something much more important. :

The previous weekend, Dennis told me, he was listening to a
presentation on the "Revisionist Roswell" The presenter discussed the new
dates on which key events allegedly took place. When the talk turned to
Dennis's experiences at the Roswell AAF hospital, Dennis suddenly and
forcefully knew with absolute certainty that the revisionist dates were wrong
and what the correct ones were. He told me it was one of those unbidden,
blinding flashes of recall which all of us sometimes have, bringing back to
mind a bit of information which no amount of conscious effort had previously
succeeding in calling up. Previously, like every other credible Roswell
witness, Dennis could say no more than that he remembered things took place
in early July 1947. Now, something in the presentation—he has no idea what--
triggered a clear recollection which made it possible to pinpoint the dates of
his experiences.

Dennis is absolutely certain it was the afternoon of Monday, July 7,
when he received the calls about body preservation and caskets and he
transported the injured soldier to the hospital, there encountering his friend
the Army nurse and quickly running into trouble with the redheaded Army
officer and his black sergeant. The next day, Tuesday, July 8, Dennis's father
woke him up very early to tell him about the sheriffs warning, and just before
noon Dennis met with the nurse, who fearfully told him the story of the
‘strange bodies.

How can Dennis be so certain about the dates? Late in the afternoon of
the day he met with the nurse for what would be the last time, he picked up
the Koswell Daily Record as it was delivered to the Ballard Funeral Home.
Emblazoned across the top of the front page was this headline:

RAAF Captures Flying Saucer On Ranch in Roswell Region
The date on the masthead was, of course, Tuesday, July 8, 1947.
