About noon, Haut contacted Roswell's two radio stations and two
newspapers. The story quickly was put on the air and phoned in to the

program director George Walsh, who unequivocally says Haut gave him the
announcement over the telephone. About the same time, KGFL's Frank Joyce,
in his capacity as a United Press Association stringer, filed the story with UP
by Western Union[19]

National and international media interest was massive and built rapidly.
Soon the Roswell AAF public relations office, the local epee: the sheriffs
office, and KSWS were swamped with calls.[20] Associated Press headquarters
in New York directed the Albuquerque bureau to pursue the story, and the
bureau chief sent reporter Jason Kellahin and wire-photo technician and
photographer R. D. (Robin) Adair to Roswell[21]

Securing the Bebris Site

While the world press pursued the story, CIC Captain Cavitt and his senior
agent, M.Sgt. Lewis ("Bill") Rickett, who died in November 1992, drove to the
Foster Ranch to inspect the debris field In interviews with several
investigators, Rickett recalled that a number of military policemen and the
base provost marshall were on the site, controlling access to it[23] Rickett
remembered seeing only the foil-like debris and mentioned its peculiar
characteristics of unusual lightness and strength. He also said, "There wasn't
very much of it, maybe forty or fifty small pieces," and attributed cryptic but
pointed comments to Cavitt and one of the MPs which made it clear this was a
hush-hush matter.[24]

Possibly on the same afternoon, July 8, William Woody and his father, in
the hope of finding the "meteorite" they saw fall to earth a few days before,
also headed north from Roswell, driving up U.S. Highway 285. The elder
Woody was confident he had a good idea where the object fell, somewhere
southwest of and close to the intersection of Highway 285 and State Route
247, the road to Corona. The Woodys were surprised to discover armed
soldiers blocking all access off 285 from about 20 miles north of Roswell to at
least Ramon, about 60 miles north of town. At Ramon, the Woody's turned
around and headed home.[25]
