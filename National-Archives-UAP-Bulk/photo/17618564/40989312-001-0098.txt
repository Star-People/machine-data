Chapter Twenty Four
Palm Beach and McGraw-Hill
In the late 1960s and early 1970s, Palm Beach County in Florida was a hot-bed of racial
tension. Athelstan was intimately involved in the integration movement in Palm Beach.
He was asked by Governor Claude Kirk to help bring about the inevitable change as
peacefully and easily as possible; but, the intellectual climate was not easy for him to
endure. This boredom, however, caused him to produce a powerful and lasting contribu-
tion to the world of arts and science.
"In 1969 we sold our house in Philadelphia and went down to Palm Beach. I had the idea
that the small pursuits I'd always regarded as hobbies would take up my time. Apart
from my major passions, like the Experimental City and the Sea Grant, I always had time
for my hobbies.
"I found I was flying often to Washington to get somebody to talk to. Of course, I did
have friends in Florida, and Governor Claude Kirk appointed me to the State Marine
Commission. Nevertheless, ennui was quickly setting inỊThen a wonderful thing

but he'd come to the World's Fair and saw what I had done there. Wallace Harrison was
more than an architect. He was a great artist as well-often the two don't go together.
*Wallace said, 'We have an interesting assignment. Rockefeller Center is spreading
across Sixth Avenue, the Avenue of the Americas, and McGraw-Hill is building a
skyscraper there. According to the concept of Rockefeller Center, buildings must be set
back from the street-there must be a sunken plaza. McGraw-Hill has expressed an
interest, because of the nature of its business, in having a plaza that has a scientific motif
or a tinge of science to it. I told them there's only one person I knew who could do it,
and that was you."
"Harrison asked me if I would do it, and I said, 'Certainly,' because I wasn't really doing

"In starting this project, I thought first of the idea of having nature designs in sculpture,
