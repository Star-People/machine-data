Dear Mr. Schuren:

My response to your request for a printout of all the balloon launches by the Air Force [Army]
from the town of Alamogordo during July, 1947 is that I have no records of any such flights.
Further, I have no knowledge of any flights ever being launched by the Army Air Force from the
town. Enclosed for you, however, is a section of the Summary of Constant-Level Balloon Flights
carried out by members of the New York University Balloon Group from various locations,
including several from the Alamogordo Army Air Field in June, July and September, 1947.

As to your wonder as to why the Air Force is resolutely sticking with the balloon explanation [for
the debris found by W. W. Brazel, the rancher who made the original report], one viable answer is
that it is and was the truth. The neoprene meteorological balloon and radar target clusters that we
launched in early June, 1947 used new design radar targets which had not been flown previously
in New Mexico although many AAF weather personnel knew about them. Our purpose in flying
them was not for making meteorological measurements but rather was in support of a highly
classified attempt to monitor Soviet nuclear tests at very great ranges. However, when the debris
was shown to Army weather officers, they immediately and correctly identified as it being from
Army meteorological equipment [although they did not know its source because neither the
military personnel at Roswell nor at Fort Worth were cleared for or had a "need to know" about
our operations].

In early June, 1947, one of our service flights [possibly undocumented NYU Flight #4 ] was
tracked to the Capitan Peak/Arabela area about 75 miles northeast of Alamogordo AAF before it
was lost while still air borne, less than 20 miles from where the debris was recovered. In my
opinion, this flight is a likely candidate for the source of this debris.

To my knowledge, William Moore and Stanton Friedman have not "researched" these early
classified flights. I have talked with both of them but they both appeared to have already reached
firm conclusions about the incident and were not seriously interested in my information.

If, in your forty plus years of researching this incident, you have uncovered any better evidence
concerning the origin of the debris that initiated the Roswell AAF press release, I would
appreciate your writing to tell me about it.

Sincerely,
If) CAB HE
