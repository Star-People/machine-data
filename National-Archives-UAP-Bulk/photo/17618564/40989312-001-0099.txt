"I developed this idea of the Plaza of the Solar System, which went through many
contortions, and then I thought of my maps of the Earth that were the first to show the

"Then I thought of how New York's children live on land, so I did the obverse of the
world ocean and showed the huge land islands in terrazo on the pavement of this plaza,
which thousands of people traverse each day as they come out of the subway.
"The second feature was the solar system. The pool was designed to represent the sun
and the planets, built to the same scale were polished stainless steel globes, set around the
pool. The planets themselves are in diametrical proportion to the pool which represented
the sun. There is a big Jupiter and a big Saturn, and Earth is a little baseball. They're
strung around, from Mercury nearest the Sun right out to remote Pluto; but, of course,
these distances are not to the same scale as the size of the model planets. If they were,
Pluto would be miles away even on the model; but, that's the idea of the pool of the solar
system.
"The dominant feature of the Plaza was the Solar Triangle. I looked at this area of
concrete canyons and thought, wouldn't it be nice to have a sun dial of some kind in
there? So I designed a huge one. Actually, I didn't invent that, I stole it. I remembered
seeing a great brick sun dial just south of New Delhi, an old astronomical observatory
that slopes up and has curved horns, and I took the idea from that.
"For the final part of the sculpture, I came down to this isosceles triangle, designed by
nature, not by Spilhaus. When someone looks up the lowest side, it points exactly to the
position of the sun in midwinter, while the steepest side points to the position of the sun
in midsummer. The hypotenuse points up to the position of the sun at the spring and fall
equinoxes.
"Wally Harrison loved the rudiments of the idea. We went in for a conference with the

realize we don't see the sun? It doesn't penetrate to the bottom of the man-made can-

possibly see it'
"The sculpture came complete and whole on a flatcar from Cleveland and had to be
