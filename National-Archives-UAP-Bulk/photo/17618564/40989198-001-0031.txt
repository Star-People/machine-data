On June 30

the

shuttle escape studies left
off in examining state of the art systems.
As a result

escape

program manager

issued instructions to the Kennedy Space Center
improve
their ground egress systems and procedures and to the Orbiter
Project Office
to work with the prime orbiter contractor

escape options.
Included are manual and rocket assisted bailout,
rocket extraction, ejection seats and ejection pods. These
studies will define cost and schedule impacts for each concept
and will be used to help the shuttle program manager decide what
sort of
egress/escape system will be implemented before the next
flight, as well as the
longer term.
Additionally, reviews are currently underway in the areas of
shuttle hardware
concerns, intact abort capabilities (those

landing
field), and potential enhancements. The obvious intent
of all these reviews is to determine those
changes which can be
implemented in the interest of decreasing the probability of
failures which might require crew escape prior to loss of vehicle
control or ditching.
In summary, Mr. Sperry's concern for crew safety is shared by
NASA, and
a significant effort is currently underway both to
decrease the probability of catastrophic failures which would
require
escape
and to provide the crew with some reasonable means
safely leave the vehicle should a non-intact abort emergency
arise.
