When the moon passes in front of the
sun, we call it a solar eclipse. But the moon
may also pass in front of a star or a planet.
That is called an occultation. These events
are not uncommon; there are plenty of
stars along the moon’s path in the sky,
so every night a few are thus occulted.
Generally the stars are faint—not even
bright enough to be seen without the help
of a telescope. An occultation of a very
bright star is much more unusual, and so
is an occultation of a planet.

However, the moon occults Saturn on the
evening of Sept. 10, and it will be visible
all over the United States and Canada. At
least the moon will be up when it happens,
but in the states and provinces along the
West Coast the sun will not yet have set.
This will make it impossible to observe
without a telescope.
