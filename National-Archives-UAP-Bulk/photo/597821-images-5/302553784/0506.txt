STATEMENT
On 1 Aug 66 while enroute to a storm reconnaissance area in the South China
Sea our navigator noticed that someone was tracking our aircraft on radar.
We were East of the Phillipines at this time, on a heading of about 270.
While looking to the left of our track to possibly see the aircraft we thought
would be making the interference on our radar, the pilot saw a group of lights
moving in the opposite direction as our aircraft.
There were three lights in
the group moving at a very fast rate of speed. These lights varied in color
from white to light red.
We followed them visually for about five (5) minutes.
I beliave I was the last to see them as they disappeared behind our plane.
While watching the first group our pilot spotted another group quite a bit
further to our left moving in the same direction as we were. This as well as
the first were in a step line like formation with the lead one being the lowest.
This group also changed color the game as the first. Once in awhile two (2)
of them would draw closer together and then separate again. We watched these
for about fifteen (15) minutes before resuming our primary mission. At one time
we turned toward the lights to possibly get a little closer but decided thay
were too far off our original track.
While scanning all around the aircraft for anymore possible sightings I saw a
very bright light behind and the right of us at about our five (5) o'clock
position.
It was all white and very bright. I called the Co-pilot and noti-
fied him of this.
He turned the aircraft far enough to the right to see this
light and verified its presence.
After landing at Clark AB, P.I., the Pilot, Co-pilot, Navigator and Weathei
licer de-briefed about the sighting with the security people thers.
They
had also radioed the sighting from the aircraft before continuing our flight.
FRANCIS W. HENRY JR., SSSt, USAF
Flight Examiner, Dropsonde Operator
