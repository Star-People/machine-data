This was clearly not a hallucination since four people saw it, and
the first impressions vere in rapid succession of patrol cars with
blinking lights and then as the object got closer, of an airliner about
to crash. There seems to be no doubt in their minds as to the very large
angle subtended by the object. They indicated that had it come closer, it
might just have been able to fit in between the successive telephone poles
along the road.
Personally, I would like to entertain the helicopter hypothesis,
but cannot unless the a) angular size is in error; b) the lack of noise
is explained; and c) there is an explanation for the absence of landing
lights. Still, we should check to see whether a helicopter mission was
perating out of Triax Airbase that night.
We should also check the weather records. The witnesses' recollection
is that the night was calm and that a lighted scientific balloon could not
be the explanation. Seientific balloons, as I well know, if they descend
nearly to the ground, do not suddenly take off and rise rapidly.
Unfortunately, this sighting must remain in the unidentified category,
in my opinion, unless drastically new informetion is received. It occurs
to me, that had this been a daytime sighting, we might have had something
similar to the Socorro sighting except, of course, for the total absence
of noise. It differs also, of course, in that there was no actual landing.
This case, therfore, joins the group of unidentifieds which seems to grow
et a steady (and distressing) rate. I believe it would be profitable to
revisit the scene, but this time with the mother and daughter, and go over
the same ground in the daytime. Veather records for that night should certainly
be obtained. It is especially important to know whether there was an
overcast, and what the wind conditions were.
