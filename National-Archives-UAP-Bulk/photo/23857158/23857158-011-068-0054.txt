SUPPLEMENTARY INVESTIGATIVE EFFORTS:
1. The first step in the follow-up investigation was the personal interview
with the four SOURCES in Gover, Missouri. This resulted in their completing ATIC
Form l64, upon which Parts One thru Four of this report are based. There were two
other witnesses to the sighting. They were a Mr. and Mrs
#of Gower. How-
ever, they were not availabls to investigators and had seen the sighting with the
tour SOURCES. They had arrived at the post at 10:00 PM CST to take over the watch
from the two women.
2. No check was made by investigators of any flight services. This was not
done because of a telephone conversation with lst It. Levine of Detachment 5,
4715th Ground Observer Squadron. He reported that he received notice during the
sighting from the Gover GOC Post. He stated to investigators that he immediately
checked with his radar and other normal sources to determine if any aircraft were
in the air near Gower. He claimed he received a negative reply. It. Levine also
checked the following air services and operations offices: Topaka, Forbes AFB,
Rosecrans, Crandview APB and CAA at St. Joseph, Missouri.
Captain Stephan of 20th Air Division (Def) was contacted with regard to
weather balloons. He stated that the only balloons which he knew of which might
have produced such an effect were radiosonde balloons. He further calculated the
winds and suggested the only station which might have released such a balloon that
could have been in the Gower area was the Topeka Weather Bureau at 2100 hours
7 August 1956. The stations in this general area which could have released radio-
sonde balloons were Cmaha, Nebraska; Columbia, Misscuri; Smoky Hill AFB, Kansas;
wichita, kansas; and Springfield, Missouri. A call was placed to the Topeka Weather
The Bureau reported its balloon was released on time and at 2145 hours was
in the area of Tonganoxie, Kansas.
4. Grandview Weather at Base Operations gave as the closest winds aloft
10,000' 200/20; 16,000' 280/09; 20,000' 280/08; 30,000' 250/30; 50,000'
station Topeka, The readings there were: Surface 090/21; 60001 200/30 260/16;

5. A call to the U.S. Weather Bureau at Kansas City for temperature readings
resulted in being referred to its veather correspondent in Edgerton, Missouri.
The correspondent maintains only maximum, minimum and 1800 hours readings. These
were 91°, 670 and 85°F.
6. The sheriff of Clinton County, Mr. Sloan of Plattsburg, reported that there
were no fixed nor temporary searchlights in the area of Gower, nor were there any
In the east which could be seen from Gover at any time during the two months
preceding the sighting.
7. A check with the 790th AC&W Squadron at Kirksville AF Station, Missouri,
was made. The Operations NGOIC, Sgt. Sullivan, stated that there was no record of
any unusual radar sightings for the evening of 7 August 1956.
Since there are no astronomers in the Kansas City area, no check could be
made with such a person regarding celestial bodies.
