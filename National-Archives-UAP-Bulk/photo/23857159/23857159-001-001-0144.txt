ll. Comments of preparing officer: The three unidentified objects were
sighted by Major Ploshay at about 2200. He immediately notified his wife and
they observed the objects together for approximately 20 to 30 minutes. During this
time there was no movement of the objects, however there seemed to be a vertical
rod or similar object slowly passing back and forth in front of each object similar
to a pencil passing slowly accross the face of a lighted bulb.

Both Major Ploshay and his wife are familiar with lights from flying aircraft
including the exhaust glow from jets at night and are positive that these sighted
objects were not the results of any known aircraft lighting. The observers com-
pletely discounted the possibility that the sighted objects were stars or planets.

The observers retired at approximately 2230. At that time the objects were
still visible, having remained in their original position. At 0200 Mrs. Ploshay
arose and again looked for the objects but they had disappeared.

The following night, 8 November 1954, at approximately 2230 (081330Z) hours,
Major Ploshay again looked for the objects sighted the previous night and was sur-
prised to see them as before, except that they were smaller as though at a greater
distance and the orange object to the left at 280 degrees had moved closer in to
the other two blue objects to a position at 250 degrees. The blue objects remained
at 270 degrees and 310 degrees respectively. At 2245 the object at 510 degrees
dropped down to approximately 20 degrees in elevation, swung right to 315 degrees
azimuth and climbed back to the altitude of the other two objects (30° elevation).
The three objects then appeared to be going away from the observer and at the same
closing the interval between them as the object to the right and left converged
on the center one. As they started moving the orange object turned bright blue,
the same color as the other two. They disappeared from sight in approximtely one
minute at 270 degrees.

Major Ploshay established the angle of elevation and azimuth by sighting over
known land marks. from his position and then taking a compass reading on the position
of each object.

Weather conditions 081330Z was approximately the same as reported in paragraph
7b above, except that visibility was 8 miles.

Weather balloons are released at 1800 and 2400 nightly, thereby eliminating
the balloon observation lights as the sighted objects.

The 529th AC & W Group did not have any unidentified radar plots during the
periods of this report.

Crews of aircraft airborne during the time of this report sighted no unusual
or suspicious lights or objects.
