At 1630 hours (local time), 16 March 1953, Mr Ernest Hawk and Mr Charles A. Stern
were watching an AF B-4’7 when their attention was drawn to a noisless, metallic ob-
ject executing maneuvers at an altitude about two-thirds the altitude of the B-47.
These maneuvers were being performed in and around a few scattered clouds. The speed
of the object was estimated as about 3 times that of the B47. (Acheck with the Max-
well Weather Station revealed that at the time of the sighting there were scattered
clouds at 20,000 feet. A further check with the Maxwell Radio Range revealed that an
AF B—4'7 #8355 from MeDill AFB, Florida was in the area at 35,000 feet with a speed of
425 knots.) Mr Stern states that could not give an accurate description of the object,
but it appeared to be about the size of an F+80. Both observers state that at times

the object appeared to be round, then again, when viewed from another angle, it look-—
ed like it had delta or swept-back wings. As the B~47 passed overhead it left distinct
vapor trails, but the object observed left no vapor trails and showed no signs of pro-
pulsion. This object performed maneuvers that are impossible for known conventional
aircraft to perform. Both observers agreed that the object seemed to maintain a con-
stant speed through such maneuvers. They described the object as climbing, diving,
executing right and left angle turns and reversing its course without turning at an
angle or ina circle.

Subject sightings were made by the naked eye. The observers were located on the
ground at Raimer, Alabama (32 03"30"N, 86°13'30"W). Observers state that they were
about 15 miles south of the object and viewed the object from a 45° angle. This ob-
ject departed the area on a 360° course at approximately 1650 hours. The total time
of sighting was approximately 20 minutes.
