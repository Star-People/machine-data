The following information was obtained by long distance telephone

1. As close as I could say it was elliptical; a dim white light
compared with a ceiling light or the light a search light would throw on
the bottom of a cloud. It was moving. It is possible that it could have
been a search light; however, it moved back and forth in a limited arc.
As far as I could tell, there was no overcast. As far as I know, the only
thing that would throw up a light like that is the airport at Tulsa, okla.,
approximately 70 miles away. The most objects I saw were two (2), one of
which was intermittent.
One was directly above the other. There is a
possibility of a trail.
The uppermost light seemed to disappear occasion-
ally. There was a very limited range of movement, I would say a 10° arc.
I heard no noise. If these objects were at the distance I think they
were, the movement would have been quite rapid, I would say somethere be-
tween 5 and 750 mph. This is based on what I have observed from aircraft.
I left the objects to make the call to report them; when I went back they
were not there. From time to time I saw stars above the object. I have
never seen anything like this before.
I saw this object at approximately 2300 (CST) on 6 July 1953•
I observed this for approximately two and one-half (22) minutes.

horizon. It was about 120 North. The A & M Campus is located in the
North Central part of Stillwater, Oklahoma.
