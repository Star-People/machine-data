At 1945%, 1 March 1953, the Trenton Filter Center received a report of an un-
identified object over Observation Post at Princeton, New Jersey. The observer,
Mr. Norman Fowler of 3 Franklin Terrace, Princeton, New Jersey, reported a circular,
silver, stationary object, location: Alfa Lina 2120 at 40,000 plus feet. His
report relayed immediately to ANDC.

position. At 2100%, relieved of duty, Ist It Richard F. Watson departed for
Princeton.
Examination of this object with the naked eye revealed a star or planet-
like object at approximately 250° with approximately 70° elevation.
Through bi-
noculars the object appeared to change shape and brilliance; at times resembling
a first quarter moon. Experimentation with the focus of the binoculars, however,
indicated that movement and change of shape were actually the result of
refracted
light in the lens.
Heturning to Trenton at 23002, It. Watson was able to locate
this object at approximately 260° and elevation 70° est.
This fact leads observer
(It. Watson) to the conclusion that the object in question was actually Venus or
some other celestial body.
