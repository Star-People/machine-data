Description: On March 24, 1976, the manager of a restaurant notified the gendarmerie that two bright spheres with an approximate diameter of one meter had just been discovered on the beach of La Feuillère (Marie-Galante Island, Guadeloupe). The gendarmerie actually observes two spheres buried in the sand at the limit of the breaking wave and partially covered by the sea water. They are 14 meters apart from each other. A light metallic coating (approximately 5/100 of a millimeter) covers both spheres: on one of them, the coating appears to have melted, revealing a harder and more resistant metal that does not scratch with the point of a knife. The following day at 6:30 a.m., the gendarmerie cleared the objects, and it was found that they consisted of two hemispheres belonging to the same craft and fitting perfectly together. An inscription H 476 AC is readable on one of the hemispheres. These craft were to be returned to the brigade for cleaning and possible identification. But at 10:00 on the same day, the mayor of Capesterre de Marie-Galante, despite the ban by the gendarmerie, will have these hemispheres removed to display them on the town hall square. This case is not an observation of PAN. It concerns the collection of two metallic hemispheres on a beach. The origin of these hemispheres might perhaps be found by using the nomenclature found inside one of these hemispheres.
